01 / Scope and objectives

About the workshop

Networked intelligent systems increasingly process continuous and heterogeneous data generated by sensors, wearable devices, biomedical platforms, microphones, cameras, radar systems, and industrial equipment. In many applications, the receiving entity does not require the complete source data, but a representation containing sufficient information for a specific downstream task.

The 1st Workshop on Learning to Extract and Send Semantics (LESS 2027) focuses on the extraction, representation, communication, and evaluation of task-sufficient application-level information derived from real-world data streams, considering the complete chain from physical observations and source data to semantic representation, network communication, and downstream application decisions.

LESS brings together semantic, goal-oriented and task-oriented communications, application-driven sensing, Edge AI, distributed inference and network-application co-design. The emphasis is on identifying the downstream application's actual information requirements and validating that the extracted and communicated representation is sufficient.

02 / APPLICATION-GROUNDED EVALUATION REQUIREMENT
CORE SUBMISSION REQUIREMENT

A concrete application is
required, not illustrative.

Every submission must be grounded in a concrete and credible application scenario. An application presented only as motivation is not sufficient. Authors are expected to establish what the downstream application actually needs and to measure the consequences of communicating a derived representation rather than the original observations.

Each paper is expected to make the following explicit
  1. 01

    Source observations

    Which physical measurements, signals, traces or original source data are available?

  2. 02

    Application & requirements

    What downstream task is being performed? Which information does that task require?

  3. 03

    Extracted representation

    How is task-relevant information extracted and represented (e.g., event, state, intent, feature or embedding)?

  4. 04

    Communicated vs. omitted

    What is sent to the receiver, and which source-level information is not communicated?

  5. 05

    Application-level performance

    How well does the receiving application accomplish its task using the communicated information?

  6. 06

    System-level impact

    What changes in communication, computation or overall system operation, and what trade-offs are observed?

NOT SUFFICIENT ON ITS OWN

A generic semantic encoder evaluated only through compression, reconstruction or an abstract similarity score, with an application merely cited as a possible motivation.

APPLICATION-GROUNDED CONTRIBUTION

A wearable sensing system specifying which movement variable a downstream task needs, extracting it from IMU data, and evaluating task performance together with transmitted information and processing costs.

Physical deployments are encouraged but not mandatory. Real datasets, traces, prototypes, testbeds, emulation environments and realistic system models are all welcome when the application-level evaluation is credible.

03 / Topics of interest

Technical scope

Contributions are invited on the following topics, among others, when the proposed method or system is tied to a concrete application scenario:

01

Information requirements & semantic representations

  • Application-grounded semantic communication
  • Application-level semantic extraction
  • Task-sufficient semantic representations
  • Goal-oriented and task-oriented communication
  • Semantic interfaces between applications and networks
  • Representation of application information requirements
  • Semantic extraction from continuous sensor streams
  • Event-, state-, intent-, token-, feature-, and embedding-based communication
  • Multimodal semantic representations
  • Semantic filtering and relevance-aware communication
02

Edge intelligence & network architectures

  • Edge AI for semantic extraction
  • Device-edge-cloud semantic processing
  • Distributed and split inference
  • Application-aware and semantic-aware networking
03

Adaptation, uncertainty & network mechanisms

  • Semantic freshness and Value of Information
  • Uncertainty and confidence in semantic representations
  • Adaptive semantic representations and semantic update generation
  • Semantic sufficiency metrics and interoperability
  • Processing and prioritization of semantic traffic
  • Mechanisms for requesting additional or source-level information
04

Benchmarks & experimental validation

  • Benchmarks for application utility versus communicated information
  • Experimental systems, datasets, testbeds, and real-world deployments
04 / Application domains

Representative applications

Digital health, wearable systems, sports sensing, speech and audio, industrial IoT, robotics, smart environments, intelligent transportation, autonomous systems, and integrated sensing and communication. Contributions from other domains are welcome when they clearly establish the application task and its evaluation.

05 / Paper submission

Submission guidelines

PAPER CATEGORYFull papers only
MAXIMUM LENGTH7 pages
SUBMISSION SYSTEMEDAS · link to be announced

Authors are invited to submit original research papers presenting technical methods, systems, prototypes, experimental studies, or evaluations relevant to the scope of LESS 2027. Every paper must include a concrete application scenario with application-level evaluation, as specified in the mandatory application-grounded requirement.

Only full papers are accepted, with a maximum length of seven (7) pages. There will be no short-paper or demo-paper tracks. Papers will be submitted through the LESS 2027 EDAS subconference of ICIN 2027. The direct EDAS submission link and final manuscript-format instructions will be published when that entry is configured and approved.

As specified by ICIN 2027, workshop papers will be included in the same proceedings as main-conference papers. Specific author and publication procedures will follow the official ICIN guidance.
